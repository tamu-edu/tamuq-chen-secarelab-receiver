# Critique of 1D v47 Theory, Outputs, and Entries

Date: 2026-09-01  
Scope: `1D_v47.jl`, `run_1D_v47.jl`, `test/smoke_1D_v47.jl`, `summaries/theory_1D_v47.md`, `summaries/journal.1D.md`, and `summaries/1D_v47/*`.

## Executive assessment

Version 47 is a meaningful provenance and diagnostic improvement over v46: the saved parameter vector reproduces the recorded objective, the smoke suite passes, the T3 zero-flow pathology is improved, the core axial drop error is lower, and the energy ledger explicitly reports storage as the steady-flux gap. However, the theory and journal entries overstate what has been resolved. Several key scientific claims remain unsupported or technically inconsistent, especially the grid-invariant heat-transfer coefficient claim, the interpretation of volumetric dominance, the Nusselt entry formulation, the rear axial conductance units, geometry provenance, material-property synchronization, and radiative/optical identifiability.

The current v47 artifacts should therefore be treated as a reproducible calibration snapshot, not as a validated physical model for extracting macroscopic heat-transfer coefficients.

## Main technical findings

### 1. The grid-invariant HTC claim is not supported

`summaries/theory_1D_v47.md` states that the reported heat-transfer coefficient is a flow-weighted or heat-flux-weighted quantity of the form:

```text
sum(h_i * |Q_i|) / sum(|Q_i|)
```

The runner implementation instead computes a temperature-difference-weighted quantity:

```text
sum(h_i * |DeltaT_i|) / sum(|DeltaT_i|)
```

The distinction is material. A mesh check shows that the reported quantities are not grid invariant:

| Case | Nodes | Arithmetic mean h | DeltaT-weighted h | Q-weighted h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| E67 | 15 | 90.2 | 341.5 | 480.5 |
| E67 | 25 | 115.6 | 570.1 | 1053.7 |
| E67 | 50 | 164.7 | 973.3 | 2907.9 |
| E72 | 15 | 112.0 | 454.7 | 725.1 |
| E72 | 25 | 149.9 | 734.5 | 1582.9 |
| E72 | 50 | 221.8 | 1186.8 | 4257.9 |
| E81 | 15 | 42.9 | 64.7 | 67.7 |
| E81 | 25 | 45.5 | 92.5 | 110.0 |
| E81 | 50 | 51.1 | 160.9 | 279.5 |

Temperatures shift only modestly, but the inferred coefficients change strongly with node count. This invalidates the current theory statement that the reported HTC is a stable macroscopic extraction.

For example, the first core source cell receives approximately 81.16% of the core source, and the cumulative source reaches approximately 93.48% by 11 mm. First-cell coefficients are also very large, for example about 1537 W/m2/K for E67 and 2314 W/m2/K for E72. The journal statement that downstream negative convection has been eliminated is therefore false.

### 3. The Nusselt entry correlation is internally suspect

The v47 Nusselt formulation applies an entry term of the form:

```text
Nu_entry = (0.0668 * Gz) / (1 + 0.04 * Gz^(2/3))
Nu = A + Nu_entry * (Re * Pr * Dh / zstar)^B
```

Because `Re * Pr * Dh / zstar` is itself a Graetz-like group, this multiplies one Graetz dependence by another. Near small axial distance and with `B = 0.9283`, the behavior approaches a high-order inlet singularity. This is consistent with the observed mesh sensitivity and first-cell dominance.

The fitted lower bound `A = 1.0` is also physically questionable as a fully developed square-channel baseline. The code allows `NU_FLOOR = 0`, but the additive lower-bound term is still active.

### 4. Rear axial conductance appears dimensionally inconsistent

The rear axial coupling is documented as a conductance in W/K, but the implementation divides by `dx`:

```text
Q = (G_rear_axial / dx) * (T_neighbor - T)
```

This is dimensionally inconsistent unless the parameter is actually a conductance-length product. It also introduces mesh dependence. Either the model equation or the parameter documentation must be corrected before treating `G_rear_axial` as physically interpretable.

### 5. Geometry provenance is unresolved

The background geometry notes document a corrected rear-tube/cavity configuration based on user/CAD inputs:

```text
rear tube length = 150 mm
cavity radius = 75 mm
metal thickness = 18 mm
insulation radius = 57 mm
adaptor = 77.6 mm x 57 mm
gas radius = 6.5 mm
wall thickness = 1.5 mm
T3 axial position = 140 mm
```

The v47 code uses a different geometry set:

```text
felt thickness = 25 mm
case thickness = 30 mm
rear tube length = 63 mm
inner radius = 7 mm
outer radius = 11 mm
```

Unless these are newer verified measurements, v47 appears to have regressed toward older or fabricated geometry. The theory document mostly tracks the code, but does not establish the provenance of the geometry revision.

### 6. Material-property synchronization is not true

The v47 code redefines material-property functions after including earlier model code. It uses exponential SiC conductivity and simplified heat-capacity/air functions, while earlier synchronized references use polynomial property forms and different solid density. Therefore the journal/theory claim that material properties are synchronized with the earlier corrected implementation is inaccurate.

This matters because conductivity, capacity, and density affect the same observables being used to infer transport coefficients.

### 7. The fitted optical/radiative parameter is confounded

The same fitted `beta` parameter controls both Beer-Lambert source absorption and Rosseland radiative conduction. This conflates optical extinction and effective radiative diffusion into a single calibration degree of freedom.

Representative decomposition shows the impact:

| Temperature, K | Conductive k, W/m/K | Rosseland radiative k, W/m/K | Radiative fraction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 | 2.70 | 0.20 | 7% |
| 900 | 0.90 | 1.14 | 56% |
| 1200 | 0.40 | 2.70 | 87% |

Because temperature data alone cannot cleanly identify source absorption and radiative diffusion when both are governed by the same fitted coefficient, the fitted `beta` should not be interpreted as a validated optical extinction coefficient.

### 8. Flow-dependence errors remain large

The wall inversion sign matches only 5 of 15 heating cases, approximately the same as the current v46 manual output. Core axial drop improves relative to v46, but remains substantial at about 39.34 K mean absolute error.

The heating flow-slope residuals remain poor. Example v47 slope errors by power group:

| Power group | T8 | T12 | T11 | T9 | T10 | T3 | T2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 456 W | -7.50 | -17.76 | -24.49 | -18.30 | -21.34 | -19.43 | -1.26 |
| 304 W | -11.73 | -14.98 | -18.09 | -15.08 | -15.46 | -13.50 | -0.92 |
| 256 W | -11.49 | -10.09 | -10.39 | -10.39 | -8.51 | -6.28 | -0.51 |

This is a central limitation for a model intended to extract heat-transfer coefficients across flow conditions.

### 9. Cooling diagnostics are not yet a blind validation

The cooling cases are held out from the heating calibration objective, but the cooling simulations initialize from measured core/perimeter/tube/T2 profiles. This makes cooling a constrained relaxation test rather than a fully blind forecast.

Additionally, the `t90` cooling diagnostic appears to use a rising-threshold criterion. For decaying cooling traces, this can return zero-like crossing errors and make the diagnostic overly optimistic. The cooling endpoint metric is more meaningful than the current `t90` implementation.

### 10. Energy conservation is improved but narrower than stated

The smoke test shows the main thermal network balances to numerical precision for representative cases. For E67, the endpoint ledger closes with near-zero residual after accounting for storage:

```text
delivered input = 220.59 W
suction = 32.39 W
core gas = 107.93 W
rear gas = -24.72 W
total gas = 115.60 W
front radiation = 11.01 W
cavity = 9.19 W
flange = 54.73 W
storage = 30.06 W
residual ~= 0 W
```

However, the T3 observer is a one-way diagnostic state. Its natural-convection/radiation exchanges are not subtracted from the gas/tube network, and the energy audit excludes this observer state. Therefore the conservation claim should be limited to the main model network, not the whole augmented observer formulation.

### 11. Parameter identifiability remains weak

The best recorded run stopped by `MaxTime`. Several parameters sit at or near bounds:

```text
A = 1.0 lower bound
B = 0.9283 near upper bound
h_suction = upper bound
G_rear_cavity = lower bound
fcore_rear = near upper bound
```

The model has many fitted degrees of freedom relative to the available observables, and v47 does not yet provide multistart convergence evidence, profile likelihoods, confidence intervals, posterior correlations, or a systematic identifiability analysis.

The reported effective heat capacity of 302.74 J/K matches the target, but this is not independent validation because capacity regularization is included directly in the calibration objective.

## Documentation inconsistencies

The following theory/journal statements or tables should be corrected before using v47 as a reference document:

- The heat-transfer coefficient formula in theory does not match the runner implementation.
- The claim of grid-invariant HTC extraction is contradicted by mesh tests.
- The journal claim that downstream negative convection is eliminated is false.
- The theory conclusion that all critiques are addressed is false.
- The optimization return code `MaxTime` and objective value `1.8023` should be reported explicitly.
- Theory parameter bounds do not match the code for several parameters, including `B`, front loss, gas heat-capacity scale, perimeter capacity, perimeter conductivity, `beta`, axial scale, rear capacity, delta, `Cz3`, and `A`.
- The water reference temperature is documented as 293.15 K, while the code uses 298.15 K.
- The external natural-convection coefficient is documented as 5 W/m2/K, while the code uses 8 W/m2/K.
- T3 position is inconsistently described as 140 mm while model/plot usage maps it to a different location.
- The theory's reported parity "mean error" table does not match the signed steady means computed from the exported CSVs; the metric definition is unclear.
- The theory's circular-area/porosity arithmetic appears internally inconsistent: `A = 3.6305e-4 m2` does not match the stated porosity when compared with the coded square footprint.
- The claim that all 15 heating and 3 cooling cases have complete timewise residual proof is stronger than the exported artifacts demonstrate.

## Pending work

1. Replace or rederive the Nusselt entry formulation so it has the correct asymptotic behavior and does not create mesh-dependent inlet singularities.
2. Define a macroscopic HTC metric before calibration, then test it for mesh convergence and physical interpretability.
3. Fix the rear axial conductance units and rerun mesh sensitivity after the correction.
4. Resolve geometry provenance against the latest CAD/user measurements and document a single authoritative geometry table.
5. Synchronize material properties with the chosen reference implementation, or document and justify any deliberate departures.
6. Separate optical absorption from Rosseland/radiative diffusion, or explicitly state that the single `beta` is an effective confounded parameter.
7. Correct the cooling `t90` diagnostic for decaying signals and label cooling as initialized relaxation rather than blind prediction.
8. Add multistart/profile-likelihood or comparable identifiability evidence before claiming parameter extraction.
9. Extend the smoke suite to cover mesh sensitivity, output/documentation consistency, bounds consistency, all-case energy ledgers, and coefficient-reporting definitions.
10. Update the theory and journal entries to remove overclaims and align formulas, bounds, constants, and diagnostics with the current code.

## Research conclusion

v47 is reproducible and operationally better documented than v46, but it does not yet resolve the main scientific limitations. Its strongest contribution is improved bookkeeping: the parameter vector, objective, energy ledger, and smoke validation are now more coherent. Its weakest point is physical interpretation: the fitted coefficients are highly sensitive to grid resolution, dominated by inlet behavior, confounded across optical and radiative roles, and affected by unresolved geometry and material-property inconsistencies.

The next defensible step is not another broad calibration run. The immediate need is formulation cleanup and invariance testing, especially for the Nusselt entry law, the rear axial conductance, and the definition of the reported macroscopic heat-transfer coefficient.
