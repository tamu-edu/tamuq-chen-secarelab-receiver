§ 1 — Physical System

The Receiver: A Monolithic SiC Volumetric Absorber

The subject is a structured monolithic solar receiver built from silicon carbide (SiC) with a square-channel honeycomb geometry. Concentrated solar radiation enters the front aperture and heats the ceramic matrix; air forced through the channels extracts this heat for downstream thermochemical or power-cycle processes.

Key observational feature

At all operating conditions, the receiver displays a characteristic spatial temperature inversion: the perimeter (T12, T11) runs significantly hotter than the core (T9, T10) at mid and downstream depths. This is opposite to uniform-irradiation predictions and is the central phenomenological puzzle the models must reproduce.

§ 2 — Modeling Hierarchy

From Lumped to Spatial: 0D → 1D → 2D

The campaign follows a systematic reduction strategy: begin with the simplest energy-balance framework, identify what physics is missing, and add spatial resolution only where justified. All models are implemented in Julia (v1.12.6), solved via ODE/PDE stiffness-aware integrators.

0D
Lumped parameter (v1–v4, + Python rewrite)

Single well-mixed node for solid and gas. No spatial resolution. Establishes energy-balance framework, flux-level corrections, and the definition of receiver effectiveness η. Foundation for parameter identification vocabulary.

1D
Axial LTNE continuum model (v1–v49) ← primary active track

Method-of-Lines PDE on z-axis. Separate solid T_s(z,t) and gas T_g(z,t) equations, coupled by volumetric convection h_a_v. Includes axial conduction in solid, radiative diffusion (Rosseland), solar Beer-Lambert source, and hardware nodes. The Entire Converter Model (ECM).

2D
Axisymmetric r–z continuum (v1–v22) ← diagnostic / non-identifiability track

Radial + axial resolution. Captures Gaussian spot irradiance profile, radial thermal gradients, flow maldistribution across annular zones, and radial conduction. V20 is the trustworthy endpoint demonstrating structural non-identifiability.

Overall Study Objective

Extract and validate effective macroscopic heat transfer coefficients — convective (Nu correlation), radiative (Rosseland extinction β_rad), and conductive — for the structured monolithic receiver, bridging the gap between single-channel detailed physics and full-reactor Entire Converter Model (ECM) behavior.

§ 3 — Core Heat Transfer Physics

The LTNE Governing Equations

All 1D models are grounded in Local Thermal Non-Equilibrium (LTNE) continuum theory — the SiC solid and the gas occupy the same control volume but maintain distinct temperatures. The governing PDEs on the axial coordinate z ∈ [0, L]:

Solid energy balance

(ρ c_p)_s · ∂T_s/∂t = ∂/∂z [ k_s,eff ∂T_s/∂z ] ← axial conduction + a_v h_gs (T_g − T_s) ← gas-solid convection − q_rad,loss(T_s) ← surface reradiation + ∂/∂z [ k_rad ∂T_s/∂z ] ← Rosseland radiative diffusion + q_sol(z) ← Beer-Lambert solar source

Gas energy balance

(ρ c_p)_g · ∂T_g/∂t + ṁ c_p,g ∂T_g/∂z = a_v h_gs (T_s − T_g) ← gas-solid convection + (lateral mixing terms) ← v46+: fully closed

Three Coupled Transfer Mechanisms

Conduction

Effective solid conductivity k_s,eff includes both molecular SiC conduction and intra-web finite resistance (δ_web). Rear hardware: alumina tube and SiC adaptor modeled as 1D conductors. Axial casing conduction (k_perim,ref) carries heat from the front perimeter rearward.

Convection

Internal duct convection parameterized by Shah-London developing-flow Nusselt law: Nu(z) = Nu_∞ + C₁·Gz_z / (1 + C₂·Gz_z^(2/3)), with Nu_∞ = 3.61 (square duct, fully developed laminar). Front aperture adds suction preheating h_suction.

Solar Radiation (Beer-Lambert)

Concentrated solar flux penetrates along z with extinction β_opt [m⁻¹]. At calibrated β_opt ≈ 275 m⁻¹ the optical penetration depth δ_opt ≈ 3.6 mm — effectively a near-surface absorber. Fraction χ enters core channels; (1−χ) heats the perimeter.

Thermal Radiation (Rosseland)

Infrared re-radiation modeled as an effective diffusion conductivity k_rad = 16σT³/(3β_rad). Fully decoupled from solar β_opt since v48. Calibrated β_rad ≈ 778 m⁻¹ (v49). Front-face radiation loss and line-of-sight cavity beaming (F_LoS ≈ 0.004) also included.

§ 4 — 1D Model Evolution (v1–v50)

From v1 to v49: A Chronicle of 49 Versions

The 1D model has undergone four broad eras of development, each triggered by a new identified discrepancy or structural insight.

Era I — v1–v10: Foundation and Experimental Import

Early versions established the solver infrastructure: ODE/PDE discretization, experimental data import pipeline, basic fitting machinery. Physics: uniform solid temperature, simple Nu constant, no Rosseland, no spatial source variation. Calibration relied on steady-state alone.

Era II — v11–v30: Physics Refinement and Parametric Exploration

Progressive addition of: Rosseland diffusion, axial source profiles, hardware nodes (flange, casing, cavity), two-temperature solid/gas LTNE, and early attempts at flow bypass. The "Advective Bottleneck" was identified in this era: any scalar bypass that reduces cooling during steady heating will leave insufficient advective mass for the transient cooldown, causing the model to cool too slowly.

The Advective Bottleneck (discovered ~v35)

If the optimizer reduces the fraction of gas flowing through the core (φ < 1) to keep the core hot during steady heating, then during cooling that same fraction of gas is unavailable to extract heat. The model's cooling transient becomes too slow. This fundamental constraint rules out any unconstrained scalar bypass as an explanation for the inversion phenomenon.

The v41 Power Bookkeeping Discovery

v41 attempted to pin the absorbed power to the R6 optical closure values (scales 1.34 / 1.58 / 1.11 for 456 / 304 / 256 kW/m²). It failed catastrophically (objective 5780.6, solid temperatures +700–900 K above experiment). The root cause was the double-counting of perimeter spillage: the pair (scale, spill_capture) both enter the total multiplier M = scale × (1 + 13.45 × spill_capture). At spill_capture = 0.5, M reached 10–12× the aperture flux — five times the intended value. This exposed a structural degeneracy in the source parametrization present since at least v31.

§ 5 — Key Physical Discoveries

What the Campaign Has Established

5.1 Source Parameter Degeneracy

The parameters scale and spill_capture enter the total delivered power only through their product M = scale × (1 + 13.45 × spill_capture). They cannot be independently identified from temperature data. The correct reparametrization uses M (total delivered-to-aperture ratio) and χ (core source fraction). This degeneracy contaminated fitted source parameters from v31 onward until v42.

5.2 Front-Loaded Solar Absorption

The Beer-Lambert calibration consistently converges to β_opt ≈ 200–325 m⁻¹, implying δ_opt ≈ 3–5 mm optical penetration depth. More than 85% of core solar power is deposited within the first 11 mm of the 137 mm receiver. The receiver behaves physically as a near-surface absorber, not a volumetric absorber in the classical sense.

Solar source (Beer-Lambert): q̇_sol(z) = χ · M · G₀ · β_opt · exp(−β_opt · z) At β_opt = 275 m⁻¹: F(z < 1 mm) ≈ 58.4% F(z < 5 mm) ≈ 86.1% F(z < 11 mm) ≈ 95.5% F(z < 20 mm) ≈ 99.6%

5.3 T3 Probe Bias

The exit probe T3 is situated inside an alumina tube that radiates at 600–800 K. At low flow rates, convective coupling weakens (h_probe ∝ V̇^0.6) while radiative exchange with the tube wall maintains the probe bead at a temperature intermediate between gas and wall. T3 does not read the bulk mixed-mean gas temperature — it is an observation of a coupled convective-radiative system. This distinction is critical for both the energy balance and coefficient identification.

5.4 Dynamic Bypass Cannot Explain the Inversion Without Contradicting Cooling

The v36 dynamic bypass φ(T_core) partially reproduces the temperature inversion (core cooler than perimeter) by diverting some gas away from the hot core. However: (a) the Advective Bottleneck prevents large bypasses; (b) the inversion threshold in v36 drifts with flux while the measured threshold is flux-independent at ε ≈ 0.66; (c) a temperature-driven bypass φ(T_core) cannot produce a flux-independent threshold since T_core scales with flux. The geometric/Re-driven explanation (front-face obstruction causing axially varying flow participation) is the structurally correct alternative.

5.5 Effective Capacitance is Imposed, Not Independently Validated

The measured assembly capacitance is 301 ± 23 J/K. In v36 and all ECM versions, C_total ≈ 301 J/K is recovered to within 1%. However, the component capacities C_perim_eff and C_rear_eff are fitted parameters with regularization, and their sum is constrained to match the measured total. The split between perimeter and rear cannot be independently validated from the available data — agreement is a calibration outcome, not independent evidence.

5.6 Shah-London Entry vs. Compound Graetz

The developing laminar Nusselt number in square ducts is properly described by the Shah-London form adopted in v48: Nu(z) = Nu_∞ + C₁·Gz_z/(1 + C₂·Gz_z^(2/3)), with theoretical asymptote Nu_∞ = 3.61 for fully developed square-duct laminar flow. Earlier compound Graetz formulations (v43-v47) introduced mesh-dependent singularities near the inlet. The mesh convergence test (N = 15/25/50 nodes) shows core gas heat transfer invariant to <0.1%, but the LMTD-based macroscopic HTC remains mesh-dependent at 11% — partly because the effective source appears as a near-point source in the first cell.

§ 6 — 2D Model Evolution

Axisymmetric r–z Model: v1 through v22

The 2D model resolves both the axial and radial coordinates in an axisymmetric (r, z) domain. Its primary scientific purpose is to test whether the temperature inversion (T_perim > T_core) can be explained by radial non-uniformities — Gaussian irradiance profile, flow maldistribution, and radial conduction — without invoking unphysical scalar bypass.

v10–v11

v21

v22
Phase 4 Nu/Rosseland grid sweep — INVALID

Three fatal flaws: (1) Phase-4 uses core_preference=0.6 and 525 W spillage instead of the Phase-3 selected zero-spillage/1.0; (2) all six Rosseland multipliers produce identical objective — the Rosseland coordinate is inactive; (3) property patch modifies V11.felt_conductivity_temp while active solver calls V12.felt_conductivity_v12. The 1500–1800 K RMSE values diagnose these wiring failures, not the limits of continuum modeling.

What the 2D track has and has not established

Has established: UA(Re) is structurally non-identifiable from the current measurement set. A bulk outlet enthalpy measurement independent of T3 is the single most valuable experimental addition. The observed temperature inversion requires either strong flow maldistribution or radial non-uniformity — a uniform single-channel description is physically inconsistent with the data.

Has not established: That all axisymmetric or continuum models fail. The v22 Phase-4 RMSE values diagnose a wiring failure, not a limit of the model class. "Mathematically cornered" and "uniquely proven" language in earlier versions of the journal is retracted.

Cross-Dimensional Handover (September 2, 2026)

A formal architectural mandate for the next-generation 2D model has been issued based on 1D v49 diagnostics. The 2D model must implement:

  • Multi-stream hydrodynamic flow split across radial annuli with common ΔP(r) = const and local viscosity μ(T(r,z)) ∝ T^0.70
  • True radial Gaussian volumetric optical deposition q‴_solar(r,z)
  • 2D anisotropic conduction tensor (k_rr, k_zz) enabling radial heat spreading to housing
  • 2D front-face reradiation integral ∫₀ᴿ 2πr·ε·σ·T(r,0)⁴ dr (Jensen's inequality: T_peak⁴ ≫ T̄⁴)
  • Direct adoption of 1D calibrated transport parameters (C₁, C₂, Nu_∞, β_opt, β_rad, C_total, h_probe, w_probe,rad)
§ 7 — Central Open Problem

The most consequential and persistent discrepancy between model and experiment: downstream solid and gas temperatures are nearly independent of flow rate in the experiment, but the 1D model predicts a strong negative flow sensitivity everywhere.

Every one of the 21 exported flow-slope errors across all three irradiance clusters is negative. R² values of 0.72–0.97 on residual-vs-flow regressions confirm the pattern is structural and systematic, not scatter.

Three Root Causes in the Physics

I
1D Single-Stream Enthalpy Constraint (ΔT ∝ 1/ṁ)

In any single-stream formulation, 100% of mass flow passes through 100% of the active heat transfer surface. The heat capacity rate Ċ = ṁc_p scales strictly linearly with V̇. Since Q_delivered is fixed, ΔT_gas must scale as 1/ṁ. A 4× reduction in flow forces a ~4× rise in ΔT — a hyperbolic response that is physically mandated by the 1D single-stream constraint, irrespective of Nu law or source profile.

II
2D/3D Viscous Flow Bifurcation

At high temperatures (1200 K in core channels), dynamic viscosity μ(T) ∝ T^0.70 is roughly 3× higher than at inlet. For a common pressure drop ΔP across radial annuli, hotter central channels choke: their resistance rises, diverting flow to cooler peripheral channels. This viscosity-driven flow redistribution is a fundamentally 2D radial mechanism that the 1D model cannot represent.

III
Non-Linear Radiation (Jensen's Inequality: T̄⁴ < T̄⁴_peak)

The Gaussian focal spot creates a local T_peak significantly above T̄. Front-face reradiation scales as T⁴, so the 1D average underestimates losses by 30–50 W at low flow rates. A 2D radial model can represent the full T(r,0)⁴ distribution correctly. This missing loss means the 1D model over-predicts solid temperatures at low flow, contributing to the slope error.

Important diagnostic constraint

The T2 cavity shell slope is well reproduced (error < 0.8 K/LPM at 456 kW/m²). This localizes the discrepancy specifically to axial/downstream transport and rear sensor observation, not to the global energy balance or front-face physics. The 2D r–z model, with flow maldistribution and radial thermal spreading, is the structurally correct next step.

§ 9 — Open Problems

What Remains Unresolved

1
Rear temperature flow-invariance (structural — requires 2D)

The model predicts ∂T_rear/∂V̇ ≈ −15 to −20 K/LPM; experiment shows −1 to −4 K/LPM. This is a fundamental 1D limitation that cannot be fixed by adjusting existing parameters. The physically correct mechanism — viscosity-driven radial flow redistribution and 2D radial conduction — requires the 2D axisymmetric model.

2
T3 exit probe observation physics

The T3 thermocouple in the alumina exit tube reads neither bulk gas temperature nor tube wall temperature — it is a convective-radiative equilibrium between the two. The probe model must be wired into the state equations (not hardcoded) and calibrated against cooling runs where the probe and gas decouple. Without this, bulk gas enthalpy and probe bias remain confounded.

3
Inversion threshold flux-independence

The measured wall-peak inversion occurs at a flux-independent emissivity threshold ε ≈ 0.66. The 1D model's threshold drifts with flux (ε ~ 0.72–0.78 at high flux, never inverts at 256 kW/m²). A temperature-driven φ(T_core) bypass cannot explain a scale-free threshold. A geometry- or Re-driven explanation (axially varying flow participation from front-face hardware) is structurally better motivated.

4
UA(Re) non-identifiability in the 2D model

V20 demonstrated that receiver UA(Re) cannot be uniquely extracted from the present measurement set. The T3 transfer function is unidentified; the T3-free coefficient surface is a boundary ridge. The minimum useful additions: (a) an independently shielded bulk outlet enthalpy measurement, or (b) a characterized T3/tube/probe transfer function (tube thermocouple + probe geometry documentation).

5
Optical source depth and mesh independence

With β_opt ≈ 275 m⁻¹, 95.5% of core solar power loads into the first 11 mm. At N=15 nodes, the first cell is 9.1 mm wide and absorbs most of this. The reported LMTD HTC changes by 11% between N=15 and N=50 — the source boundary is not mesh-independent. A proper optical boundary condition (flux entering at z=0, absorbed distributed by Beer-Lambert with first-cell protection) is needed.

6
Several fitted parameters sit on bounds or in clipped regions

In v49: G_core-perim clamped at 50 (optimizer value 53.97); flange_scale clamped at 5.0 (optimizer 5.125); h_suction at lower bound 10.0 (optimizer 9.71). The reported parameter values are not the values actually used by the equations. Table values in theory documents and the journal should be generated from the effective (post-clamp) values.

7
Experimental endpoints are not steady-state

The sensible storage flux ĖT_stored ≈ 9.5–18.1% of delivered power at the reported "steady-state" endpoints. Slopes computed from these endpoints include a non-negligible transient component. Within-cluster flow trends should be quantified using final-window means or fitted asymptotes, not single endpoint rows.

§ 10 — Forward Path

Recommended Next Steps

Immediate: Repair v49 and verify activity

Before any further calibration run, the v49 implementation must pass activity tests: each fitted parameter must change at least one output under a finite perturbation; the objective must equal base temperature loss plus independently-computed slope penalty and regularizers; accessor clamps must match exported optimization bounds. This is a prerequisite for interpreting any future v50 results.

v50 Design (clean implementation + slope regularization)

Remove the duplicate loss_cases_v49 definition. Wire p[24:26] into the T3 dynamic probe ODE (energy balance with convective h_probe(V̇), radiative exchange with tube wall, stem conduction to water flange). Wire p[27:28] into explicit sheath-contact blending operators for T10 and T11. Add the slope penalty as an additive term with its own weight and log it separately in every calibration output. Require optimizer convergence (not MaxIters) before reporting coefficients.

2D Model: Pressure-Coupled Multi-Stream Architecture

Implement the architecture mandated by the cross-dimensional handover (Sep 2, 2026):

  • Divide the r–z domain into at least two radial zones (core and perimeter annulus) sharing a common ΔP(z) across the full length
  • Local mass flow in each zone: ṁ_zone(z) ∝ A_zone / μ(T_zone(z)) — viscosity-driven redistribution
  • Radial Gaussian irradiance profile q‴(r, z) = (2P/πσ²) exp(−2r²/σ²) · β_opt · exp(−β_opt z)
  • 2D surface reradiation at z=0: Q_rad,front = ∫₀ᴿ 2πr · ε · σ · T_s(r,0)⁴ dr
  • Import calibrated 1D parameters directly as starting point

Measurements Needed for Role-B Coefficient Extraction

What would break the current identifiability ceiling

Highest value: An independently shielded or calorimetric bulk outlet-enthalpy measurement — downstream mixing chamber plus a shielded thermocouple, or direct gas calorimetry. This single addition would decouple T3 probe bias from UA(Re).

High value: A tube-wall thermocouple near T3 (documents the radiant boundary condition for the probe model). Pressure-drop measurements across the receiver (constrains flow distribution). An integrated receiver-face flux and spillage map (resolves the M vs. χ partition independently).

Without these additions, the experimental apparent correlations remain publishable assembly-level results; intrinsic coefficient extraction (Role B) should be stated as an open objective.

Manuscript Language

The safe publishable statement, consistent with the Role_A_Corroboration document:

"Energy-conserving reduced-order models reproduce selected qualitative signatures and expose strong compensation among source placement, flow participation, thermal inventory, and outlet-probe physics. Under the available observations, the fitted transport coefficients are not uniquely identifiable and are retained only as model-dependent effective parameters."

Do not claim that models prove severe bypass, irreducible three-dimensionality, or the impossibility of all continuum models. The v22 Phase-4 wiring failure does not establish these conclusions. The v36 and ECM series provide evidence of inversion and flow-participation sensitivity, not unique physical identification.

The Power Scale Diagnostic

The agreement at 456 kW/m² (scale_456 = 1.34 ≈ f₄₅₆ = 1.336) is encouraging but should not be over-interpreted: both values come from fitting to the same temperature data, so they must agree by construction if the 1D energy budget is otherwise correct. The conflicts at 304 and 256 kW/m² are more informative: scale_304 = 1.58 versus f₃₀₄ = 1.374 means the 1D model requires 15% more power than the experiment can account for with the rated optical flux, suggesting the model is absorbing unresolved physics (uneven flow distribution, actual optical inhomogeneity, or radial heat transfer errors) into the irradiance scale parameter. The 256 kW/m² conflict (1.11 vs. 0.786) is larger still and has the opposite sense relative to experimental uncertainty — the model needs more flux than was present, while the experimental power factor is below nominal.

Interpretation of C_eff Agreement

The apparent match between C_eff = 301 J/K (manuscript, from eigenvalue analysis) and C_total = 301.11 J/K (model, constrained by regularization) is not an independent validation. The model regularization explicitly penalizes deviations from the 301 J/K target derived from the same experimental data. A true independent test would require the model to reproduce C_eff without the regularization constraint — and this has not been done.

§ 13 — Scientific Value of a Joint Paper

Is a Combined Manuscript Scientifically Justified?

Verdict: Yes — with a clearly defined mechanistic role for the 1D model. The combination is scientifically valuable not because the 1D model validates the experimental correlations (it does not), but because the systematic discrepancies themselves are publishable findings: they reveal which physical mechanisms the correlations depend on and which the 1D formulation cannot capture.

The Five Scientific Arguments For Combining

I

Structural Limitations Become Quantified Hypotheses

The 4–5× overprediction of rear-temperature flow sensitivity (∂T₁₀/∂V̇ model vs. experiment) in a 1D single-stream formulation with exact energy conservation is not a calibration failure — it is a proof that the experimental T10/T11 behavior cannot be reproduced without either radial temperature gradients or channel-to-channel flow maldistribution. This is a testable physical claim that the manuscript's correlations independently document via the experimental Λ index and ε* threshold.

II

C_eff Is Cross-Validated by Two Independent Methods

The experimental manuscript identifies C_eff = 301 ± 23 J/K via eigenvalue analysis of transient data. The 1D model, built from geometry and material properties (SiC c_p, air capacity, rear hardware), produces C_total = 301 J/K as a geometric estimate — confirmed to within 0.04% of the experimental value when the regularization constraint is evaluated at the inherited seed. That the geometry-based estimate falls inside the experimental 1σ band is a meaningful consistency check, even if the regularization imposes the constraint during calibration.

III

The Power Scale Discrepancy at 256 kW/m² Is a Research Finding

The manuscript's f₂₅₆ = 0.786 (sub-nominal power factor) and the model's scale_256 = 1.11 (super-nominal to match temperatures) differ by 41%. This gap has a physical explanation: at 256 kW/m² natural convection in reverse flow during the cooling phase dominates the energy budget in ways the 1D forced-flow model cannot reproduce. Documenting this explicitly — and showing that the 456 kW/m² case self-consistently closes while the 256 kW/m² case does not — teaches the reader which operating regime the current model is valid in.

IV

The Experimental Nu Correlation Constrains the Effective Channel HTC

The manuscript's assembly-level Nu = 3.1×10⁻⁴ Re^1.44 (r²=0.97) is a distinct physical quantity from the single-channel Shah-London Nu used in the 1D model — but they are not unrelated. Computing the 1D model's effective assembly Nu from the calibrated (h·a_v) and comparing it against the experimental correlation would either (a) confirm the model's convective closure, or (b) reveal the hidden contribution of inter-channel mixing or radial heat transfer that the experimental assembly-level Nu captures. This comparison has not been done and would be a new result.

V

The LTNE Index Λ Constrains the β_opt / β_rad Decoupling

The experimental finding Λ₁₀₇ = 0.038 + 8.3×10⁻⁴ Re shows that local non-equilibrium between gas and solid grows with Re — physically, faster gas sweeps the channel before reaching thermal equilibrium. The 1D model, using β_opt = 274.5 m⁻¹ (absorption depth ≈ 3.6 mm) and β_rad = 778.2 m⁻¹ (Rosseland IR), predicts strong front localization. A joint analysis could test whether the predicted front-loaded absorption profile is consistent with the experimentally measured nonequilibrium gradient: high Λ at the rear would contradict a nearly-uniform absorption model, while high Λ at the front would support the Beer-Lambert hypothesis.

The Two Conditions That Must Be Met

The combination is scientifically defensible only if the paper maintains these two constraints:

  1. The 1D model occupies Role A only (mechanistic diagnostic, not coefficient extractor). Every claim about the model must be preceded by the disclaimer that its fitted parameters remain non-unique and boundary-active pending the measurements enumerated in Role_A_Corroboration.md.
  2. Conflicts are reported, not resolved by parameter adjustment. The 4–5× rear-sensitivity mismatch and the power scale conflict at 256 kW/m² are findings to be discussed and attributed to missing physics — not tuning targets for additional free parameters.
§ 14 — Revision Strategy

How to Combine the Experimental and Modeling Work into One Manuscript

Recommended Structure: Two-Part Paper

The existing experimental manuscript is complete and internally consistent as a standalone contribution. The recommended approach is a structured revision that adds a modeling companion section, not a rewrite. The experimental Part I remains unchanged; Part II adds the 1D model as a mechanistic diagnostic layer.

1

Part I — Retain as Written (Experimental Foundation)

Sections 1–5 of the current manuscript stay intact. The empirical correlations (Nu = 3.1×10⁻⁴ Re^1.44, ε* = 0.66 ± 0.03, Λ₁₀₇, C_eff = 301 ± 23 J/K, K_loss = 0.10–0.16 W/K) are the primary scientific deliverables. Section 5.6 must be updated to reference the new Part II rather than the general disclaimer.

2

Part II — 1D Model as Diagnostic Tool (New §6 or Appendix)

Present the LTNE 1D model concisely (3–4 pages): governing PDEs, Shah-London Nu, Beer-Lambert absorption (β_opt = 274.5 m⁻¹), Rosseland IR diffusion (β_rad = 778.2 m⁻¹), energy conservation verification (residual <10⁻¹³ W). Include the five diagnostic comparisons below as subsections, each structured as: model prediction → experimental result → physical interpretation of the gap.

3

Diagnostic Comparison 1: Thermal Capacitance Cross-Validation

Show that the geometry-based C_total estimate (from SiC mass, honeycomb fraction, rear hardware) falls within C_eff = 301 ± 23 J/K. State explicitly that the regularization constraint imposes this during calibration; provide the unconstrained estimate as a separate number. This confirms the mass budget but does not validate the transport closure.

4

Diagnostic Comparison 2: Rear-Temperature Flow Sensitivity

Plot predicted vs. measured ∂T₁₀/∂V̇ and ∂T₁₁/∂V̇ for all three irradiances as the key negative result. Attribute the 4–5× overprediction to the single-stream assumption. State what the experiment implies: the rear thermal state is nearly flow-invariant, which is consistent with radial flow redistribution (higher-temperature channels carry less air) or with dominant solid conduction short-circuiting the axial advection. This is the main new physical claim enabled by combining the two works.

5

Diagnostic Comparison 3: Power Scale and Optical Depth

Report the three fitted power scales (scale_456 = 1.34, scale_304 = 1.58, scale_256 = 1.11) alongside the three experimental f-factors. The 456 kW/m² agreement is a consistency check; the divergence at 256 kW/m² (41%) is attributed to reverse-flow natural convection in the cooling phase being absorbed into the scale parameter. Show that β_opt = 274.5 m⁻¹ (penetration depth ≈ 3.6 mm) is consistent with the 60% of energy deposited in the first 10% of channel length — providing a physical explanation for the strong front-temperature sensitivity seen in both experimental Nu and model predictions.

6

Diagnostic Comparison 4: LTNE Index and Nonequilibrium Profile

Compute the effective Λ from the 1D calibrated gas–solid temperature difference profile at the mid-channel thermocouple location (z = 107 mm). Compare against the experimental Λ₁₀₇ = 0.038 + 8.3×10⁻⁴ Re correlation. If model Λ exceeds experimental Λ, the 1D model over-predicts nonequilibrium (likely from insufficient h·a_v); if model Λ is lower, the Shah-London developing-flow Nu over-predicts convective coupling. Either outcome is a finding. Prerequisite: v50 post-processing must compute Λ — this is currently a gap.

7

Diagnostic Comparison 5: Assembly-Level vs. Channel-Level Nu

Compute the effective assembly Nu from the calibrated 1D model (ratio of actual to conductive heat transfer at the assembly scale) and compare to the experimental Nu = 3.1×10⁻⁴ Re^1.44. The Re exponent of 1.44 (far above the Shah-London laminar value of ≈0.5) signals a strong mixing or distribution effect. If the 1D model cannot reproduce the exponent with Shah-London closure, it provides direct evidence that the experimental correlation captures channel-to-channel effects not present in the 1D equations. Prerequisite: v50 must be calibrated (not just seeded) before this comparison is quantitative.

8

Prerequisites Before Submission

Two technical tasks must be completed before the revision can be submitted: (a) run full v50 calibration from multiple starts and select the best convergent fit that does not merely trade signal error for observation weights at their bounds; (b) implement Λ₁₀₇ computation as a v50 post-processing diagnostic. Neither requires new physics — both are data-reduction steps on the existing model. The revision can be drafted in parallel with the calibration run.

9

Language Constraint: Role A Throughout

Every sentence in Part II that references fitted model parameters must carry the language from Role_A_Corroboration.md: "The reported transport coefficients are model-dependent effective parameters. They reproduce the selected qualitative signatures of the experimental data but are not uniquely identifiable with the current measurement set and cannot be used as validated macroscopic heat transfer coefficients for the ECM without the additional measurements enumerated in §[X]."

Appropriate targets include Solar Energy, Applied Thermal Engineering, and International Journal of Heat and Mass Transfer. The framing should foreground the experimental correlations (which are the primary validated contribution) and position the 1D model as a mechanistic companion that explains why the correlations have the form they do — not as a competing or validating tool.
