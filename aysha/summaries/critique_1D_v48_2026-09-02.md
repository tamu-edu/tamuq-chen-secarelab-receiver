# Critique of 1D v48 Outputs, Documentation, and Flow-Rate Trend Failure

Date: 2026-09-02  
Scope: `1D_v48.jl`, `run_1D_v48.jl`, `test/smoke_1D_v48.jl`, `summaries/theory_1D_v48.md`, `summaries/journal.1D.md`, and `summaries/1D_v48/*`.

## Executive Assessment

Version 48 is a real structural improvement over v47. It removes the most problematic compound Graetz formulation, rewrites rear axial conduction as a conductance-length term, decouples optical absorption from Rosseland diffusion, exports a clearer macroscopic LMTD heat-transfer coefficient, fixes the cooling `t90` direction, and preserves machine-precision first-law closure.

However, the v48 documentation overstates the scientific result. The saved outputs do not support the claim that v48 has resolved the rear-temperature flow-invariance problem. The model still predicts rear/core/outlet temperatures that are much too sensitive to flow rate. The added line-of-sight radiation term is numerically negligible in the fitted solution, and the axial source field remains extremely front-loaded. The current v48 should therefore be treated as a cleaner calibration experiment, not a validated coefficient-extraction model.

### 2. The Line-of-Sight Radiation Mechanism Is Too Small in the Fitted Solution

The fitted line-of-sight view factor is:

|---|---:|

| E72 | 0.008 |

These values are far below the rear gas reheating and flange/tube heat-flow scales. They cannot be the claimed rear thermal floor. In the current fitted model, line-of-sight radiation is present in the equations but not important in the energy budget.

### 6. Cooling Validation Remains Constrained by Measured Initial Conditions

The cooling `t90` direction is corrected in `run_1D_v48.jl`, but cooling is still initialized from measured core/perimeter/rear/T2 profiles. It is therefore a constrained relaxation test, not a blind thermal forecast. The high mean absolute `t90` errors also indicate that the transient dynamics are not yet strongly validated.

### 7. Wall/Perimeter Inversion Remains Poor

The sign of `T12 - T8` matches only 5 of 15 heating cases. The model often predicts the perimeter mid-station colder than the front wall when experiment has the opposite sign, or vice versa. This is important because the rear flow trend cannot be fixed independently if heat is being assigned to the wrong radial or perimeter reservoir.

## Heat-Transfer Interpretation: Why the Flow-Rate Variation Is Hard to Capture

The experimental trend is physically specific: the front responds strongly to flow, while downstream/rear measurements are comparatively flow-invariant. A single-stream 1D enthalpy-marching model naturally struggles with this because changing mass flow acts globally.

In v48, the gas is marched through one representative channel bundle:

```text
NTU_i = h_i P dx / (mdot cp)
Tg,out = Ts - (Ts - Tg,in) exp(-NTU_i)
Qgas_i = mdot cp (Tg,out - Tg,in)
```

As flow changes, the same `mdot` and Reynolds-dependent `h` alter every axial cell. There is no independent degree of freedom allowing front cooling to increase while rear thermal state remains almost fixed. Once the gas stream has been cooled or heated differently near the inlet, that enthalpy history is carried downstream.

Several physical effects can break this coupling in the real receiver but are weak or absent in v48:

1. **Nonuniform channel flow distribution**. Real honeycomb channels may not share the imposed flow equally. Edge channels, central channels, blocked channels, entrance maldistribution, and manifold asymmetry can let high-flow paths dominate front convective cooling while low-flow or radiatively heated regions dominate rear thermocouple readings.

2. **Thermocouple transfer functions**. T3, T10, and T11 may not be pure local gas or pure local solid temperatures. Stem conduction, sheath radiation, off-axis placement, contact resistance, and finite junction response can flatten measured flow dependence relative to the actual gas enthalpy field.

3. **Three-dimensional rear hardware conduction**. The rear tube, flange, adaptor, casing, and thermocouple attachments form a sparse 3D heat-spreading network. A single axial rear rail cannot represent azimuthal spreading, local contacts, or parallel thermal paths.

4. **Radiation geometry is not a scalar end-to-end view factor**. If radiative exchange matters, it is likely distributed wall-to-wall, channel-to-channel, and wall-to-hardware, not just a first-node to last-node correction. The fitted v48 `Q_rad_LoS` is too small to enforce the rear floor.

5. **Source placement and flow response are confounded**. The fitted source is extremely front-loaded. That makes the downstream field sensitive to how the gas redistributes inlet heat. Without independent outlet enthalpy or optical absorption data, the inverse problem can trade source depth, `h(Re)`, radiation, and rear conductance against each other.

6. **Observed flow invariance may be an integrated measurement effect**. The rear sensors may average a mixture of solid, gas, radiation, and hardware temperatures. A local 1D state variable will then have a steeper flow response than the measurement.

The practical conclusion is that the rear flow trend is not likely to be fixed by another scalar HTC correlation alone. The model needs either additional observability or additional spatial structure.

## Ranked Suggestions

### 1. Add an independent outlet enthalpy or T3 transfer constraint before claiming HTC extraction

Highest priority. Without an independent gas outlet enthalpy measurement or a defensible thermocouple transfer model, `UA(Re)`, source depth, rear conductance, and radiation remain confounded. Treat T3 as an observation model with position, sheath exchange, radiation, and stem loss uncertainty rather than as direct gas temperature.

### 2. Replace the single-stream 1D gas model with a minimal multi-path flow model

Test a two- or three-path model: central channels, edge channels, and leakage/peripheral paths, each with its own flow fraction but sharing a coupled solid/hardware network. The target is not arbitrary bypass; it is to allow front convective cooling and rear thermocouple response to decouple in a physically constrained way.

### 3. Build a rear hardware/sensor submodel before further tuning `G_rear_*`

The rear trend is probably controlled by tube/flange/adaptor/contact geometry and thermocouple coupling. Replace the single rear rail with a small network: receiver exit face, adaptor/tube wall, flange contact, casing/cavity, and sensor sheath nodes. Put geometry-derived priors on conductances.

### 4. Downgrade line-of-sight radiation claims unless its heat-flow scale is demonstrated

Keep `F_LoS` as an exploratory mechanism if useful, but do not claim it resolves the rear invariance while it contributes less than 0.2 W. If radiation is suspected, implement distributed radiative exchange or a bounded radiative-conductance network and report its W-scale contribution.

### 5. Revisit optical source placement with independent priors

The current source field deposits about 92% of core solar power in the first cell. That is effectively a surface receiver. If the receiver is meant to be interpreted as volumetric, the optical source law needs independent support from optical characterization, ray tracing, or a bounded sensitivity study.

### 6. Add flow-slope terms directly to the calibration/reporting gate

The existing objective can improve RMSE while still missing the physical trend. Add explicit slope penalties for `T10`, `T11`, and `T3` within each irradiance group, or at least promote them to hard reporting gates.

### 7. Tighten mesh-invariance tests and align documentation to the actual threshold

If the theory claims less than 5% macro-HTC spread, the smoke test should assert that value. If 15% is the accepted engineering tolerance, the theory should stop calling the coefficient grid-invariant.

### 8. Correct documentation synchronization

Update the journal values for `beta_opt`, `beta_rad`, `F_LoS`, heat capacity, artifact count, and optimizer return code. Replace "fully converged" with the actual `MaxIters` status.

### 9. Use v48 as a cleaner baseline, not a final model

v48 is valuable because it removes several avoidable mathematical problems from v47. It should be the baseline for controlled hypothesis tests, not the endpoint for manuscript-level coefficient claims.

## Research Conclusion

v48 improves the implementation quality and reduces average errors relative to v47, but it does not resolve the scientific problem of flow-rate response. The model still forces a change in flow rate to propagate too strongly through the rear thermal states. The experimental data appear to require either spatially nonuniform flow, a better rear hardware/sensor observation model, distributed radiative exchange, or independent enthalpy constraints.

The next useful modeling step is not a broader scalar calibration. It is a constrained model-form test that breaks the artificial 1D coupling between front convective cooling and rear sensor response while preserving first-law accounting and avoiding empirical flow-dependent boundary artifacts.
