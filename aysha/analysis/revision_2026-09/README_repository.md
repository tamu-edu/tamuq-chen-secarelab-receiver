# Reproduction repository — depth-resolved transients in a SiC honeycomb volumetric solar receiver

A. Melhim and K. E. Kakosimos

Every number, table and figure in the manuscript is produced by two Julia scripts from the raw
logger files. There are no hand-entered constants and no intermediate manual steps, so the archived
`outputs/` and `figures/` directories can be deleted and regenerated in full.

## Layout

| Path | Role |
|---|---|
| `receiver_reduction.jl` | Single reduction pass: reads the raw logs and writes every derived scalar, archive table and figure input |
| `make_figures.jl` | Draws Figures 3–7 and Figure S1 from that archive, and exports the three plotting trace files |
| `RAW/` | Raw logger files, one per run, as written by the instrument and otherwise unmodified |
| `outputs/` | Reduction archive (regenerated by the scripts; committed so the manuscript numbers can be checked without a run) |
| `figures/` | Manuscript figures as PNG at 300 dpi |
| `Project.toml`, `Manifest.toml` | Pinned Julia environment of the archived run |

`RAW/` holds twenty logger files: fifteen heating runs E67–E81, three cooling transients C69, C80
and C81, and the two repeat runs E82 and E83, which are reduced and archived in
`groups_replicates.csv` but excluded from every fit reported in the manuscript. The run-to-file
mapping is declared once at the top of `receiver_reduction.jl` in the `HEATING`, `COOLING` and
`REPLICATES` tables; nothing downstream re-derives it from file names.

## Running

From the repository root:

    julia --project=. receiver_reduction.jl --raw RAW --out outputs --nmc 4000
    julia --project=. make_figures.jl       --raw RAW --out outputs --fig figures

Both scripts accept `--raw`, `--out` and, for the figures, `--fig`; the reduction additionally
accepts `--nmc` (Monte Carlo realizations, default 4000), `--profile-starts` (random starts for the
fixed-profile test, default 40) and `--tamb` (the logger channels averaged for the inlet reference,
default `T15,T16`). The built-in defaults point at the development layout in which the scripts sit
in `analysis/revision_2026-09/` and the raw files in `analysis/RAW/`, so pass the paths explicitly
when working from the flat repository above.

Runtime is dominated by the two 4000-realization Monte Carlo passes and the multi-start profile
fits; the first invocation additionally pays Julia's package precompilation. Reduce `--nmc` for a
fast check — the point estimates do not depend on it, only the Monte Carlo standard deviations do.
`make_figures.jl` reads the archive rather than recomputing it, and takes seconds.

Both scripts are deterministic. Every stochastic step is seeded — `monte_carlo(seed=20260902)` and
`fixed_profile_test(seed=20260904)` — so a rerun reproduces the archived outputs. The multi-start
optimizer in `fixed_profile_test` is the one step whose last digits may differ across BLAS builds;
its reported statistics are stable well beyond the digits quoted in the manuscript.

Air properties are not tabulated in the scripts. `receiver_reduction.jl` calls CoolProp for dry air
at 1 atm through the `CoolProp_jll` binary, evaluating it once on a 1 K grid over 200–1600 K and
interpolating that grid at the call sites, which costs under 1e-6 relative error. The source and
the CoolProp version are written into `results.json` under `air_properties`, so an archived run
records which property release produced it.

## Environment

Julia 1.12.6. `Project.toml` declares only what the two scripts load: CSV 0.10.16, CoolProp_jll
8.0.0, DataFrames 1.8.2, ForwardDiff 1.4.1, GLM 1.9.5, Interpolations 0.16.3, JSON3 1.14.3,
Optim 2.2.1, Roots 3.0.4 and Trapz 2.0.3 for the reduction, PythonPlot 1.0.6 for the figures, and
the Printf, Random and Statistics standard libraries. `Manifest.toml` pins those and their 135
transitive dependencies at the versions of the archived run, so

    julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'

restores the environment exactly. The manifest carries no `project_hash`, having been extracted
from the development environment rather than written by a resolve; the first `Pkg.resolve()` or
`Pkg.instantiate()` stamps it and confirms the pinned set is self-consistent.

PythonPlot draws through matplotlib, which CondaPkg installs into a project-local Python on first
use. A machine without network access at that moment fails there rather than in the reduction,
which needs no Python: `receiver_reduction.jl` runs to completion and writes the full archive with
PythonPlot never loaded.

`figures/` and contain no reduced data.

Keys inside `results.json` worth naming, since they carry the paper's argument:

- `nusselt` — pooled and grouped (common-slope, per-configuration-prefactor) correlations, the
  square-duct references of Shah and London, and the inverse-Graetz exit coordinate
- `ntu_profile` — profile-corrected transfer units per run, their pooled and grouped exponents, the
  outlet-bias band, and the ratio to the isothermal-wall identity
- `ntu_structure` — the isothermal-wall exponent, and the requirement `d ln N / d ln Re` evaluated
  on the measured properties for a fixed Nusselt number and for a temperature-independent
  conductance
- `fixed_profile_test` — the three fixed-conductance families of section 4.2 (shared `h(z)` at
  2/3/5/7 nodes, shared `Nu(z)` at 3/5, per-configuration `h(z)` at 5), each with fitted nodes, rms
  and maximum outlet residual, the residual regression on ln Re, and the per-run residuals
- `wall_extrapolation` — the exponent under each admissible reconstruction of the unmeasured front
  11 mm and rear 30 mm
- `identification` — the four capacitance and loss determinations plus the primary
  matched-effectiveness one, with `COOL_PROVENANCE` fixing which heating run each cooling transient
  decays from
- `controller_correlation_bounds` — every Monte Carlo quantity in both controller-correlation limits
- `crossings`, `ltne`, `similarity`, `sensor_selection_swing`, `heating_window_sensitivity`,
  `T3_sensitivity`, `delivered_power`, `monolith`, `geometry`, `envelope`

## Conventions to know before using the numbers

`Re` is nominal: formed on the metered flow divided equally among the hundred channels, which is
the assumption sections 5.2 and 5.3 argue is violated. Nusselt numbers and conductances are
likewise apparent, referred to the length-averaged side-wall temperature under the quadrature
weights in `WTS`. These are the correct objects for the paper's arguments and are not validated
transport coefficients; section 5.1 quantifies how far they move when the reference probe is
changed.

Uncertainties come in two kinds and are never mixed. Instrumental terms are Monte Carlo standard
deviations over the propagated instrument model. Regression standard errors describe the scatter of
runs about a fitted law. For the correlations of section 4.2 the regression term is larger by an
order of magnitude and is the one to read as the uncertainty of the correlation.

The four mass-flow controllers are propagated as four persistent per-unit errors drawn once per
realization and combined with each record's own measured shares, so a single physical unit error
reaches every steady and transient record with weights that differ only because the shares do.
Because the manufacturer states no correlation between units, every instrumental term is computed
twice, fully correlated and independent, and both are archived.

## Known limitations of the package

The raw files are the instrument's own output and include channels not used here, among them an
irradiated probe whose mounting position is not recorded. The heating eigenvalues are reported
under two sensor rules because an earlier analysis chain applied a rule different from the one its
header documented; both are archived rather than one being chosen silently. The installed
differential-pressure channel is retained although section 4.7 shows it cannot resolve the monolith
pressure drop, because that null result is what motivates the measurement recommended in section
5.3.
