Dear Editor,

I am submitting our manuscript entitled

"What depth-resolved transient measurements on a SiC honeycomb solar receiver imply for reduced-order heat transfer closures"

authored by Ms Aysha Melhim and myself, for publication in Energy Conversion and Management. The main body is approximately 14,000 words with 5 tables and 7 figures.

Volumetric solar receivers are consistently reported to perform below the models used to design them, and those models are normally validated against an outlet air temperature with one or two solid temperatures. We instrumented a silicon carbide honeycomb receiver at nine locations, tested it in a high-flux solar simulator at three irradiance levels, and reduced its fifteen heating and three cooling transients without fitting a thermal model to them. Two results follow. The measured transfer-unit count rises with flow, as Re^+0.341, where a single-stream formulation whose volumetric conductance is a fixed function of axial position requires it to fall as Re^-1.00; because the disagreement is in sign, it is not removed by recalibration. Separately, changing only which thermocouple is taken to represent the solid moves the identified Nusselt prefactor by a factor of 29.7 and the identified capacitance from 122 to 281 J K-1, so the data conventionally used for validation cannot detect such a disagreement.

The work therefore speaks to how receiver models are calibrated and judged, which we believe is of direct interest to readers of Energy Conversion and Management working on concentrating solar power and on reduced-order thermal models more generally.
