ABSTRACT
Inverse computation of gas-solid interfacial heat transfer coefficient (hsg) in porous media has remained a 
challenging issue. This work proposes a method using data integrity of both solid (Ts) and gas (Tg) temperatures 
in porous media to resolve this issue. The data integrity is accomplished by simultaneous measurement of Ts and 
Tg developed in our previous work. hsg is approximately solved by the discretized gas energy conservation 
equation in a central difference scheme implemented with data integrity of Ts and Tg. This solution takes ad￾vantages in reducing both computation cost and numerical uncertainty since only one unknown parameter is 
directly calculated from one equation. An empirical correlation of Nu = 0.001(Re1.66Pr1/3) (R2 = 0.93) is 
established for low-Re gas flow in porous media (5 mm ≤ dp ≤ 15 mm and 13 ≤ Re ≤ 133) based on in total 54 
studied cases considering four variables of Darcy flux, particle size, porous medium, and heating temperature. 
The proposed method is validated by the consistent Nu correlations in the literature. This work gives a new 
perspective of data integrity to resolve the challenging inverse models for heat transfer in porous media.
1. Introduction
Gas-solid heat transfer in porous media under low Reynolds (Re) 
number (≤ O(102
)) conditions is an important transport phenomenon 
across energy, environmental and chemical engineering applications 
[1–3]. Gas flow in porous media under low-Re conditions exhibits a 
viscosity-dominated regime, in which the flow boundary layer could be 
significant thickened [4]. In this regime, the convective heat transfer 
reduces due to small flow velocities, while the conductive heat transfer 
becomes relatively more significant, particularly in the gas phase, which 
dominates the overall thermal resistance. The low thermal conductivity 
of gas phase leads to slower thermal response compared to the solid 
phase, resulting in temperature gradients at the gas-solid interface. This 
phenomenon renders the local thermal non-equilibrium (LTNE) model 
more suitable to describe the heat transfer behavior than the local 
thermal equilibrium (LTE) model [5,6]. For instance, experimental ob￾servations by Zheng et al. [7] and Song et al. [8] confirmed the LTNE 
mechanism for porous methane combustion and biomass combustion 
due to the significant differences between gas temperatures (Tg) and 
solid temperatures (Ts). Zanoni et al. [9] demonstrated that the LTNE 
model was essential for the accurate prediction of low-Re smoldering 
combustion inside porous media. In the LTNE model, the gas-solid 
interfacial heat transfer coefficient (hsg) is a critical parameter 
coupling the gas-and-solid energy conservation equations, as shown in 
Eqs. (1) and (2).

problem for solving hsg from the LTNE model and no analytical solu￾tion exists. The sole solution in the literature as shown in Table 1, was to 
develop the inverse computation algorithms for optimizing hsg while the 
differences between experimental and numerical temperatures were 
minimized [7,13–16,18–21], in which the most typical approach was 
called a ‘transient single-blow technique’ [13,18,19,22,23]. This tech￾nique measured Ts inside porous media and inlet and outlet ambient 
temperature (Ta) of porous media, and then hsg was optimized by 
minimizing the experimental and numerical outlet Ta. However, the 
radial heat loss for the transient single-blow technique was ignored. It 
was a critical issue that could lead to a considerable error to the estimate 
of hsg because the radial heat loss was remarkable as elucidated in recent 
work [24–26]. This critical issue has been recently addressed by Zanoni 
et al. [15], in which the radial heat loss was incorporated in the solid 
energy conservation equation (i.e. U in Eq. (2)) and estimated by the 
global energy balance.
There are two critical issues faced by the inverse computation for hsg: 
(i) high computation cost and (ii) numerical uncertainty resulting from 
both numerical computation of the governing equations and inverse 
optimization. These issues are mainly caused by the inaccessible integ￾rity data of both Ts and Tg inside porous media. Supposing data is 
integrity and both Ts and Tg can be accessible from experiments, then hsg 
becomes the sole unknown parameter and it could be analytically solved 
from only one energy conservation equation. Thus, the data integrity 
could be a novel solution to determine hsg and very promising to address 
these issues by reducing the computation cost and numerical 
uncertainty.
This work aims to resolve hsg under low-Re conditions (13 ≤ Re ≤
133) using the data integrity of both Ts and Tg measured by our newly 
developed experimental measuring technique [8]. The experimental 
setup for simultaneous measurement of Ts and Tg is firstly introduced. 
Four variables including Darcy flux (volume per unit cross sectional area 
per unit time [15], also named the filtration velocity), particle size, 
porous medium and heating temperature are considered in experiments. 
With the data integrity of both Ts and Tg available from experiments, hsg 
is directly solved from the gas energy conservation equation and 
approximated with the central difference scheme. An empirical corre￾lation of Nu with respect to Re and Pr is established based on 54 studied 
cases. Sensitivity analysis for the analytical solution of hsg with the data 
integrity is conducted. Comparisons to other empirical correlations in 
the literature and limitations of this study are discussed. This work de￾velops a promising approach from a new perspective of data integrity to 
resolve the challenges faced by the inverse computation for hsg in porous 
media.
2. Methodology
2.1. Experiment setup
The schematic diagram of the experimental setup was depicted in 
Fig. 1. The geometric parameter of the reactor was with the radius of 12 
cm and the height of 60 cm. The air supply system delivered constant￾pressure gas via an air compressor, with flow rates controlled through 
the mass flow controller (MFC-700, DESIGN TECH, China). A 3 cm layer 
of coarse rock particles was placed at the bottom to ensure uniform gas 
flow. A heating coil was placed at the bottom of reactor to heat porous 
media to a programmed temperature. Two sets of K-type TCs were 
inserted with 2.6 cm intervals into the central axis of the reactor. TC data 
was recorded by a data acquisition instrument (U2331, KEYSIGHT 
TECHNOLOGIES, USA) with a 4 second time resolution. As can be seen 
in Fig. 1, the coated TCs at the left-hand side of the reactor were used to 
measure Ts at the central axis of the reactor, whereas the coated TCs 
embedded inside porous tubes at the opposite side were employed to 
measure Tg at the central axis of the reactor. A hollowed space inside a 
porous tube was created for measuring Tg. To alleviate the perturbance 
induced by invasive measurement, the hollowed porous tube mimicked 
the porous property of porous media in the reactor through peripherally 
opening several holes in the tube (see Fig. 1), which allowed gas flow 
through the tube and meanwhile controlled the porosity of the tube

2.2. Approximate solution
Since both Ts and Tg were obtained, the hsg became the only un￾known parameter in the energy conservation equations. Consequently, 
hsg can be directly solved using just one energy conservation equation. In 
this study, the gas phase energy conservation equation shown in Eq. (2)
was considered, as it included ug, enabling comprehensive evaluation of 
the hsg, and excluded the extra parameter U in Eq. (1) reducing 
uncertainty.

confirms the research findings in [7,11] that the gas-solid interfacial 
heat transfer mechanism changes significantly and local thermal 
nonequilibrium would emerge while Re decreases to below 100. Com￾parisons of Nu empirical correlations shown in Fig. 3 indicates that the 
correlation established in this work is in in line with previous work such 
as Nie et al. [17] and Zanoni et al. [11]. It demonstrates the proposed 
approach with the data integrity of both Ts and Tg owns a good capa￾bility for the determination of hsg. Furthermore, taking advantages of the 
analytical solution of hsg from the gas energy conservation equation, this 
approach is more efficient by saving enormous numerical computations 
and inverse optimizations. Besides, the proposed approach avoids the 
inverse computation errors because it does not involve any numerical 
computations and inverse optimizations.
The approximate solution of hsg in this work is slightly smaller (O 
(10− 1
)) than the approximate solution of hsg in [17] and inverse 
computation for hsg with the radial heat loss in [15]. Such slight diverges 
could be attributed to measurement noises of Tg in this work, especially 
for quartz sand [8], and also associated with errors of approximate so￾lution in [17] and inverse computation in [15]. For instance, in [17], 
only one solid energy conservation equation was solved for hsg, in which 
the other unknown variable Tg inside porous media was replaced by the 
ambient temperature (Ta). Nevertheless, the Nu empirical correlation in 
this work extrapolated towards larger Re number and lower Re number 
could merge with the correlations by Nie et al. [17] and Zanoni et al. 
[15]. Note that the Darcy flux in practice such as the filtration com￾bustion could be high to ~ 0.24 m s
− 1 [15,16]. The main reason for the 
small velocities was attributed to the comparisons with the previous 
work [15,17] validating the proposed approach in this work. The 
applicability of the established correlation in this work extending to 
larger Darcy flux will be examined in the future.

experimental uncertainty and numerical uncertainty. As for the exper￾imental uncertainty, Fig. 4 shows the sensitivity of hsg to the Tg mea￾surement uncertainty. The Tg measurement error resulting from the 
porous tubes could lead to significant bias of the estimate of hsg. It in￾dicates that attention must be paid to reduce the Tg measurement error,

The numerical uncertainty is associated with the discretization errors 
of the central difference scheme, which depends on the measuring in￾tervals at the central axis of reactor. It is expected that a finer interval in 
experiments could decrease the discretization errors. Besides, the dis￾cretization issue could be solved by mesh-free numerical algorithms, 
such as physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) [32] that incorporate 
fundamental physical principles as soft constraints in model training, 
thereby ensuring that all solutions rigorously adhere to the underlying 
governing equations. The advantage of PINNs over the finite difference 
methods lies in the mesh free, which does not require a predefined grid, 
potentially providing a continuous solution field even with sparse or 
irregularly spaced data points. Then, the numerical uncertainty associ￾ated with the discretization errors of the central difference scheme could 
be avoided. This hybrid data-physics framework demonstrates superior 
capability in characterizing complex heat transfer phenomena [33], 
which might be helpful toeleminate the numerical uncertainty of the 
proposed method using data integrity to resolve hsg in the future.
4. Conclusions
Data integrity achieved by simultaneous measurements of Ts and Tg 
in porous media is implemented in a discretized gas energy conservation 
equation to approximately solve hsg in porous media for 5 mm ≤ dp ≤ 15 
mm and 13 ≤ Re ≤ 133. This solution takes advantages in simplifying 
computation with only one equation instead of two energy conservation 
equations, and reducing uncertainty with direct heat-transfer informa￾tion from local Ts and Tg data rather than deduction of hsg only from Ts 
data. An empirical correlation of Nu = 0.001(Re1.66Pr1/3) is derived, 
which is in accordance with other correlations developed in the litera￾ture. It demonstrates that the data integrity provides a new perspective 
to resolve challenges in the inverse models of heat transfer in porous 
media.
