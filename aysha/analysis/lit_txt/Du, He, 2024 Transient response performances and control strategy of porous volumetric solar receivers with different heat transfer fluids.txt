ABSTRACT
The transient behavior of solar receivers significantly impacts start-up, shutdown, and fluid outlet temperature
stability. This study investigates the transient behaviors of porous volumetric solar receivers using various heat
transfer fluids under step increases and actual solar radiation. The porous domain is reconstructed using the unit
cell structure of Weaire-Phelan. The implementation of the solar radiation process, coupled flow and heat
transfer, as well as control algorithm leverages Monte Carlo ray tracing, pore-scale simulation, and PID control,
respectively. Results demonstrate that air and molten salt porous receivers display concave and s-shaped
response curves when exposed to a step increase in solar radiation. Molten salt’s higher heat capacity compared
to that of air causes a delayed response to solar radiation variations with an average time constant of 45.9 s,
whereas the air receiver’s time constant is only 7.0 s. Furthermore, the time constant increases by 129 % as the
mass flow rate decreases from 9 × 10− 5 to 3 × 10− 5 kg/s. The PID algorithm effectively reduces temperature
fluctuation amplitudes caused by solar radiation variation. The maximum temperature offsets for air and molten
salt porous receivers are 5.9 K and 31.4 K, respectively.
1. Introduction
Solar energy is widely regarded as the paramount source for future
human societies. Concentrating Solar Power (CSP) technology offers an
efficient mechanism for the high-temperature and large-scale utilization
of solar energy [1,2]. However, the diurnal and intermittent nature of
solar energy poses challenges to the stable operation of CSP systems [3].
The transient performance of the solar receiver, a core component
responsible for solar-to-thermal energy conversion, plays a crucial role
in determining the temperature of the heat transfer fluid and the overall
thermal efficiency of the system. Furthermore, the high temperature and
steep temperature gradient significantly affects the long-term safe
operation of the receivers [4].
Volumetric absorption stands out as a promising approach for effi￾ciently absorbing and converting solar radiation by mitigating thermal
radiation loss [5]. Nevertheless, the complex flow and coupled heat
transfer processes within porous media present challenges in precisely
characterizing thermal performances, especially concerning the tran￾sient response performance of porous volumetric solar receivers.
In comparison to the volume-averaging simulation method, pore￾scale simulation proves to be an effective tool for analyzing the
intricate and detailed energy transfer processes within porous struc￾tures. Zhu and Xuan [6] conducted simulations on packed bed porous
media, investigating the influence of geometrical parameters on radia￾tion propagation, pressure drop, and thermal efficiency. A hybrid ge￾ometry with reduced front surface temperature was proposed. Du et al.
[7,8] employed X-ray computed tomography to reconstruct porous ge￾ometry and developed image processing procedures to adjust geomet￾rical parameters such as porosity and pore size. Monte Carlo ray tracing
and pore-scale simulation methods were applied to examine the steady￾state flow and thermal performance of porous volumetric solar receivers
under intensified solar irradiance. According to the spectrally selective
absorption concept, Du et al. [9] further designed a porous receiver with
molten salt as heat transfer fluid. The flow and thermal performances
were compared to the air porous receivers with pore-scale simulation
method, and the low flow resistance and high thermal efficiency were
characterized. Navalho and Pereira [10] simulated the entire solar ra￾diation transfer process, encompassing solar energy concentration in a
parabolic dish and radiation transfer within the porous foam. Chen et al.
[11] investigated influences of the optical configurations on the thermal
performance of the receiver. The decreasing absorptivity and spectrally
selective absorption designs were beneficial for improving the energy
conversion efficiency. Additionally, based on the adjoint algorithm and

Several transient experimental tests on porous volumetric solar re￾ceivers were conducted in the early studies [14–16]. However, numer￾ical simulations provide effective tools for a detailed examination of
transient thermal performance. Wu and Wang [17] developed a tran￾sient model for porous volumetric solar receivers, considering sudden
heat flux, sudden loss of heat flux, and heat flux step changes. The results
demonstrated that the reaction time of the receiver’s outlet temperature
fell within the range of tens of seconds, and a smooth change in tem￾perature and pressure drop occurred even during sudden variations in
solar radiation. In a study by Wang et al. [18], the influences of fluid
type and thermal conductivity of porous material on the transient per￾formance of porous receivers were investigated. It was observed that
fluid type had a slight impact on the inlet temperature of the porous
skeleton but significantly affected the heat transfer process of the outlet
porous temperature. Ribeiro and Lemos [19] utilized the volume￾averaging method with a local thermal non-equilibrium model to
explore the transient temperature response of porous receivers. The
findings indicated that low porosity and decreasing Darcy number
facilitated effective heat exchange between phases, leading to a quicker
stabilization of outlet temperature. Sharma and Talukdar [20] approx￾imated various operational scenarios involving linearly changing solar
flux, including start-up, shutdown, and cloud passage for porous re￾ceivers. The temperature variations over time were recorded, revealing
a delayed response at the rear part of the porous receiver. Additionally,
Cagnoli et al. [21] proposed a multi-scale analysis method, spanning
from micro-scale to macro-scale, for porous volumetric solar receivers.
The simulation of the cloud passage process involved imposing zero
solar heat flux for 20 s. Macro-scale numerical results indicated an im￾mediate response of the front surface of the porous receiver to variations
in solar heat flux, with a rapid temperature change rate of approximately
15 K/s. Similar quick temperature variation rates exceeding 20 K/s were
also observed by Reddy and Nataraj [22].
On the other hand, diverse control strategies have been employed to
regulate the temperature of solar receivers and stabilize transient per￾formance in various solar thermal applications. This involves manipu￾lating the heliostat field, receiver aperture size, and mass flow rate of the
heat transfer fluid. In a study by Popp et al. [23], a comparison was
made between model predictive control (MPC) and a PID-based control
method for regulating the temperature of a molten salt solar receiver by
adjusting fluid mass flow. Results across various cases indicated that
MPC exhibited superior performance in terms of temperature deviation
and compliance with safety standards. However, thermal efficiencies of
the receiver were nearly identical when using both MPC and PID control
methods. Abuseada and Ozalp [24] employed both PID and MPC
methods to regulate the apertures of a cavity solar receiver, aiming to
stabilize its temperature under fluctuating solar radiation. Experimental
findings demonstrated that both control methods effectively maintained
the receiver’s temperature within 5℃ of the set point under actual solar
irradiation. To address the inherent delay characteristic of PID control,
Lu et al. [25] and Wang et al. [26] applied feedforward-feedback control
strategies in parabolic trough and solar tower receivers, respectively. Lu
et al. [25] introduced a hybrid control strategy incorporating model￾based feedforward and PI feedback controllers. This strategy compen￾sated for various disturbances such as changes in solar position, ambient
temperature, and wind speed, resulting in an approximate 0.5–1 % in￾crease in thermal efficiency. Wang et al. [26] integrated an artificial
neural network (ANN)-based feedback controller with the PID feedback
controller to enhance the response speed of the solar tower receiver to
variations in solar irradiation. The hybrid control strategy effectively
limited outlet temperature fluctuations to 5℃ under actual Direct
Normal Irradiance (DNI) data, whereas this value exceeded 30℃ in the
PID-controlled system.

the transient behaviors of porous receivers with various heat transfer
fluids remain unexplored, and a comprehensive understanding of the
transient performance of porous volumetric solar receivers under actual
solar irradiance is yet to be attained. Additionally, the response char￾acteristics of porous receivers to controlling algorithms require further

2. Methodology
2.1. Weaire-Phelan porous model reconstruction
Pore-scale simulation is conducted by directly reconstructing a three￾dimensional porous structure, as shown in Fig. 1. The Weaire-Phelan
porous model is chosen and reconstructed using the periodic unit cell
reconstruction method, mainly to mitigate the substantial computa￾tional resource demands during transient simulation.
Firstly, the unit cell of Weaire-Phelan structure including its vertices’
positions and connection information is obtained based on the built-in
model of the software Surface Evolver. Then, the geometrical data of
the Weaire-Phelan structure is imported to the CAD design software such
as Inventor to create the three-dimensional entity. The 3D sketch is
drawn by the VB macros and the porous skeleton is formed using the
Insert Frame function. Finally, the Miter function is applied to mend the
gaps between the skeletons and the porosity of the porous structure is
controlled by changing the skeleton thickness. The porosity, pore
diameter, strut thickness, and specific surface area of the reconstructed
Weaire-Phelan porous model used for the following simulation are 0.8,
2.3 mm, 0.55 mm, and 1.4 mm− 1
, respectively.
2.2. Monte Carlo ray tracing method
The solar radiation penetration and solar flux distribution inside the
porous structure significantly influence the flow and heat transfer per￾formance of the receiver. Before launching the Monte Carlo ray tracing
method, the initial state of the solar radiation is determined according to
the previous study of Qiu et al. [27]. It is shown that the incident angle
varies from 0 to about 45◦. Therefore, the solar flux distribution inside
the receiver is a weighted average of distributions under different inci￾dent angles.
When considering the solar radiation penetration inside the receiver,
the MCRT method is applied due to its flexibility to the complex three￾dimensional structures. The basic concept of MCRT method is to
simulate the radiation transfer process by statically analyze the behav￾iors of a large number of independent photons. Based on the geometrical
optics assumption, the behaviors of the photons such as reflection, ab￾sorption, and transmission can be captured. The reflections in the
interface of the porous structure and the side wall of the receiver are
assured to be specular and the absorption of solar radiation only hap￾pens at the porous surface.

4.2. Parametric study of the transient response performance of the porous
receivers
The transient behavior of the porous receivers is influenced by
various factors, including the thermophysical properties and mass flow
rate. The frequently utilized porous materials, such as silicon carbide
and stainless steel, are applied to the porous structures. Both air and
Hitec porous receivers under varying mass flow rates are analyzed. The
time constant which is defined as the required time to reach 63.2 % of its
steady state temperature is selected as the evaluation index for the re￾ceiver’s transient response performance.
Fig. 5 exhibits a notable difference in time constants, indicating
quicker responses of air receivers to solar radiation variation. Within the
same inlet mass flow rate range, the average time constant for air re￾ceivers is 7.0 s, which significantly increases to 45.9 s for Hitec receivers.

4.3. PID regulation of porous volumetric solar receiver under variant
incident flux
Under real situations, the variation of incident solar radiation for the
receivers becomes more complex. Fig. 7(a) records the DNI variation
within a day, and dramatic changes can be observed due to the cloud
cover. DNI data spanning 1500 s is extracted and used to calculate the
solar radiation intensity at the receiver inlet, as shown in the enclosed
illustration of Fig. 7(a). The peak, lowest, and mean DNI values are
1084.0, 910.0, and 997.2 W/m2
, respectively.
The simulation of the outlet temperature for the porous receivers
under actual solar radiation conditions is presented, and the tempera￾ture deviation from the steady-state temperature is illustrated in Fig. 7
(b). Absent a regulation algorithm, the temperature fluctuation curves
mirror the fluctuations in DNI, displaying consistent peaks and troughs
in both DNI and temperature profiles. In comparison to the air receiver,
a notable delay in temperature response to solar radiation is evident,
resulting in an average delay of 56 s. Additionally, several minor crests
and troughs in the air temperature profile are excluded due to the rapid
changes in solar radiation and the delayed response of Hitec. Moreover,
the magnitude of the temperature oscillation for both air and Hitec re￾ceivers is detailed in Table 2. It is noted that the temperature fluctua￾tions are more evident in the air receiver.
Furthermore, the PID controller is applied to stabilize the outlet fluid
temperature by the automatic regulation of the inlet mass flow rate for
both air and Hitec porous receivers, and the temperature offsets are
illustrated in Fig. 8. Evidently, the PID algorithm demonstrates effective
control of the air receiver, showcasing only small variations with the
maximum and minimum temperature offsets at 5.9 K and − 4.5 K,
respectively. However, regarding the Hitec receiver, the PID controller
offers restricted regulation. Comparing the temperature profile in Fig. 7
(b), it is noted that the values of temperature offset decrease, with the
maximum reduction reaching 13.3 K. Hence, more effective controlling
algorithm such as feedforward-feedback control strategy may be
necessary.
5. Conclusions
Transient response performances of different types of porous re￾ceivers under step change and actual solar radiation are investigated.
The conclusions are derived as follows:
(1) Air and molten salt porous receivers exhibit concave and s-shaped
response curves, respectively, when subjected to a step increase
in solar radiation. The higher heat capacity of molten salt results
in a delayed response to solar radiation variation and prolongs
the duration to reach a steady state.
(2) The transient response of the porous receivers is significantly
influenced by the heat transfer fluid’s thermophysical properties
and mass flow rate, while the effect of types of porous materials is
not evident. The average time constant for air and molten salt
receivers are 7.0 s and 45.9 s, respectively. Within the range of 9
× 10− 5 to 3 × 10− 5 kg/s, the lower mass flow rate increases the
time constant by 129 %.
(3) Temperature profilesfollow the variation of actualsolar radiation
for both air and molten salt porous receivers, while a delay of 56 s
is observed for the molten salt receiver. The PID algorithm can
effectively reduce the amplitude of temperature fluctuation,
particularly for the air receiver. The maximum temperature off￾sets for air and molten salt porous receivers are 5.9 K and 31.4 K,
respectively.
